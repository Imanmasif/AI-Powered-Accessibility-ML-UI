# Evaluation Framework

## Study design

The paper describes a within-subject evaluation comparing two conditions:

- **Baseline:** the original interface without adaptive contrast correction.
- **Adaptive:** AdaptiveUI deployed with a personalisation profile and real-time contrast adjustment.

The primary evaluation population is visually impaired participants. A smaller sighted control group is included to examine unintended usability effects.

## Planned recruitment

The preregistered target is 36 visually impaired participants, with recruitment of up to 40 to allow for attrition or exclusions. A smaller control group of 12 sighted participants is planned, giving a total recruitment target of 52. The paper notes that the impaired sample could optionally be increased toward 50 if resources permit.

These figures are **recruitment targets described in the study design**, not evidence of the number actually recruited.

## Tasks

Tasks are designed around common low-contrast interaction problems, including:

- locating information in low-contrast text
- identifying and activating buttons or links with inadequate contrast
- interpreting warnings or error messages with poor contrast

## Primary measures

1. Task completion time
2. Task success
3. Perceived readability

Secondary measures include error rates, number of modified elements, perceptual colour distance, trust and acceptability, and qualitative feedback.

## Statistical plan

The paper specifies paired-sample comparisons for completion time and perceived readability, with non-parametric alternatives when assumptions are violated. McNemar's test or logistic mixed-effects modelling is specified for matched binary task outcomes. Effect sizes and confidence intervals are to be reported. Holm-Bonferroni correction is specified when multiple primary outcomes are tested.

## Reported evidence

The paper's conclusion states that the experiment involving visually impaired individuals reported improvement in task execution, error rates and perceived readability with AdaptiveUI. The paper available for this repository does not provide a numerical results table suitable for reproducing participant-level statistics, so this repository does not invent or infer numerical effect sizes, p-values or sample counts.
